    Stablecoins

    Why does this matter?

    A stablecoin issuer bears asymmetric risk: any misconfiguration of the issuer account — DefaultRipple, RequireAuth, TransferRate, freeze — has immediate, on-ledger consequences for every holder. Getting issuance right is not just a technical exercise; it directly governs the legal and operational relationship between the issuer and every counterparty.

    Mental model

    An issued currency on the XRPL is a bilateral credit relationship encoded in a trust line. The issuer is not a smart contract — it is an account whose configuration flags determine the rights and restrictions of every holder. Issuance is fundamentally about managing those relationships at scale.

    Key issuer flags (see Issuer Controls for full technical details):

    • DefaultRipple — allows balances to ripple across trust lines.
    • RequireAuth — restricts trust line creation to authorized counterparties.
    • GlobalFreeze / Individual Freeze — emergency controls to halt movement.
    • NoFreeze — permanently revokes the freeze capability to build holder trust.
    • Clawback — enables the recovery of tokens from a holder's account.
    • TransferRate — an on-ledger fee charged on peer-to-peer transfers.

    Reserve math for a typical issuer setup

    A fresh issuer account needs:

    Object XRP cost Count
    Base account reserve 2 XRP 1
    Signer list (3 signers) 0.2 XRP × (N+1) = 0.8 XRP 1
    Hot account trust line 0.2 XRP 1
    Additional authorized trust lines 0.2 XRP each N

    Example: cold account with 3-of-5 multi-sig + 1 hot account trust line = 2 + (5+1)×0.2 + 0.2 = 3.4 XRP minimum. Add a buffer of 2–5 XRP for fee capacity and future object growth.

    TransferRate encoding

    TransferRate is a UINT32 stored as a ratio with 1,000,000,000 as the denominator (1.0 = no fee):

    Desired fee TransferRate value Formula
    0% (no fee) 1,000,000,000 1.000 × 10^9
    0.1% 1,001,000,000 1.001 × 10^9
    0.5% 1,005,000,000 1.005 × 10^9
    1.0% 1,010,000,000 1.010 × 10^9

    The fee is charged to the sender on peer-to-peer transfers between non-issuer accounts. Transfers directly to/from the issuer do not incur the fee. Source: rippled/src/libxrpl/tx/transactors/payment/Payment.cpp.

    Trust line authorization workflow (when RequireAuth is set)

    1. Customer submits TrustSet → trust line created in lsfNoRipple + pending auth state.
    2. Issuer (or hot account via delegation) submits TrustSet with tfSetfAuth flag.
    3. Trust line moves to lsfAuth state → customer may now hold the token.
    4. To revoke: issuer submits TrustSet with zero limit; line auto-deleted when balance = 0.

    Source: rippled/src/libxrpl/tx/transactors/token/TrustSet.cpp.

    Example flow

    Regulated stablecoin minting

    1. Issuer setup
    2. Create cold account; fund to at least base reserve + signer-list + buffer.
    3. Set asfRequireAuth and asfDefaultRipple in a single AccountSet.
    4. Configure a multi-sig SignerList (e.g., 3-of-5).
    5. Disable master key (asfDisableMaster) — irreversible without multi-sig available.

    6. Source: rippled/src/libxrpl/tx/transactors/account/AccountSet.cpp

    7. Customer onboarding

    8. Customer completes off-ledger KYC/AML; their XRPL address is recorded.
    9. Customer submits TrustSet with a limit ≥ expected maximum holding.

    10. Trust line is created in "pending auth" state (lsfLowAuth/lsfHighAuth unset).

    11. Trust line authorization

    12. Hot account (authorized by cold via delegation or multi-sig) submits TrustSet with tfSetfAuth targeting the customer's trust line.

    13. Trust line transitions to authorized state; customer may now receive tokens.

    14. Minting

    15. Off-ledger: fiat received into reserve custodian account.
    16. Cold account (multi-sig quorum) sends Payment of issued currency to hot account.
    17. Hot account sends Payment to customer — no additional on-ledger supply created (supply exists between cold and hot; hot redistributes).

    18. On-ledger total supply = sum of all balances held by non-issuer accounts.

    19. Redemption

    20. Customer sends currency back to issuer (cold or hot) via Payment.
    21. Balance returns to the issuer — supply is destroyed.
    22. Off-ledger: operator releases fiat to customer's bank account.
    23. Reconcile: on-ledger total supply must equal attested off-ledger reserves.

    Reserve attestation workflow

    Periodically (e.g., daily) verify total on-ledger supply against off-ledger reserves:

    1. Fetch all trust lines via account_lines (cold account)
   → sum all non-zero balances (from cold's perspective: negative = issued)

2. Compare sum to off-chain reserve custodian balance

3. If |on_chain_supply − off_chain_reserve| > threshold → alert

4. Publish attestation hash (hash of (timestamp, supply, reserve_balance))

    Using account_lines with pagination: 

    { "command": "account_lines", "account": "<cold_address>", "limit": 400,
  "marker": "<opaque_marker_from_previous_response>" }

    Repeat until no marker is returned. Source: rippled account_lines handler.

    Emergency freeze sequence

    When a regulatory freeze is required:

    1. Cold account (multi-sig) submits AccountSet with asfGlobalFreeze.
    2. All trust lines on the cold account are immediately frozen — no peer-to-peer transfers possible for the token, regardless of individual line state.
    3. Verify freeze: account_info on cold account; Flags field has lsfGlobalFreeze set.
    4. Notify holders per legal requirement.
    5. To selectively unfreeze specific counterparties: TrustSet with tfClearFreeze on individual lines (only possible if NoFreeze was not set).
    6. Source: rippled/src/libxrpl/tx/transactors/account/AccountSet.cpp

    Non-obvious insight: GlobalFreeze freezes all trust lines on the issuer account atomically, but individual line freezes can coexist. Setting asfNoFreeze permanently removes freeze capability — useful for building holder trust in permissionless tokens, but it also removes the issuer's regulatory lever. Choose at design time.

    Try it yourself

    Using the Testnet faucet at https://xrpl.org/resources/dev-tools/xrp-faucets:

    1. Run examples/03-trust-line-and-issue/ — observe the DefaultRipple flag and trust line authorization in action. What does the trust line state look like before the issuer authorizes it? Query account_lines on the hot account to find out.
    2. Attempt to set TransferRate to 999999999 (below minimum). What result code is returned? Look up the code in the Glossary.
    3. Enable asfGlobalFreeze on the cold account, then try to send the issued currency peer-to-peer between two non-issuer accounts. What result code do you get?
    4. Compute the reserve cost for a cold account with 5 signers and 1000 authorized trust lines. Is it economically feasible for a retail stablecoin issuer?

    Security considerations

    • The issuing account's master key must be disabled after configuration; use multi-signing for any subsequent administrative changes.
    • RequireAuth should be set before enabling DefaultRipple on a regulated token to avoid unauthorized holders.
    • Freeze capability is a double-edged sword: holders may discount the token's value. Decide between NoFreeze (permissionless) and freeze (regulated) as part of the initial design — not reactively.
    • AllowTrustLineClawback is irrevocable once set. Set it intentionally as part of the issuance design.

    Common mistakes

    • Enabling DefaultRipple without RequireAuth on regulated tokens. Without it, anyone can create a trust line and hold the token.
    • Misconfiguring TransferRate. A TransferRate of 1000000000 means no fee; values must be ≥ 1000000000. Off-by-one errors cause systematic fee miscalculation across every transfer.
    • Burning supply without reconciling off-ledger. On-ledger supply and off-ledger reserves must be kept in sync and regularly attested.
    • Operating the issuer as a hot account. The issuing key should only be used for configuration and bulk operations — never for day-to-day transfers.

    Production checklist

    • [ ] Issuer account uses multi-signing; master key disabled.
    • [ ] RequireAuth is set if the token has a restricted holder list.
    • [ ] DefaultRipple is set correctly and its routing implications are understood.
    • [ ] TransferRate is validated against expected fee behavior.
    • [ ] Freeze and clawback posture is documented and communicated to holders.
    • [ ] Minting and redemption flows are auditable end-to-end.
    • [ ] On-ledger supply is reconciled against off-ledger reserves on a defined schedule.
    • [ ] Reserve attestation process is defined, documented, and scheduled.

    This checklist covers stablecoin issuance. Before production, also complete the account-security checklist in ch04-hardening/security and the node-operations checklist in ch04-hardening/operations.

    What I learned

    • Configuration is irreversible (mostly). Flags like asfNoFreeze and asfAllowTrustLineClawback are permanent. You must design your issuance model before the first transaction.
    • Cold wallets should stay cold. Moving funds from a cold issuer directly to customers is standard, but the key that signs those transactions should be managed via secure, multi-party signing (like a vault or specialized custody service).
    • Rippling is a feature, not a bug. Enabling DefaultRipple allows your token to be used in cross-currency pathfinding, increasing its utility and liquidity.
    • Trust is bilateral. A trust line isn't just "holding" a token; it's an agreement that the issuer owes you value. The lsfNoRipple flag on the customer side is just as important as the issuer flags.
